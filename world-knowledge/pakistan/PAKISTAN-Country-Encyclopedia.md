### 1.2 Name and identity

The name Pakistan was popularised in the 1930s and became the name of the independent state established in August 1947. The name is commonly explained through associations with the region's historic territories and the Persian suffix *-stan*, meaning land or place. Pakistan's constitutional name is the Islamic Republic of Pakistan.

### 1.3 Flag

The national flag has a dark green field with a white vertical stripe at the hoist, a white crescent and a five-pointed star. The green field is associated with the Muslim majority; the white stripe represents religious minorities. The crescent and star are widely explained as symbols of progress and light, though official descriptions should be consulted for formal wording.

### 1.4 National identity elements

- **National anthem:** Qaumi Tarana, written by Hafeez Jalandhari, with music composed by Ahmad G. Chagla.
- **National language:** Urdu; it is a unifying national language while many regional languages are spoken.
- **National poet:** Allama Muhammad Iqbal is widely recognised by the state and public as the national poet.
- **National animal:** Markhor is commonly designated as the national animal.
- **National bird:** Chukar partridge is commonly designated as the national bird.
- **National flower:** Jasmine is commonly designated as the national flower.
- **National tree:** Deodar is commonly designated as the national tree.
- **National sport:** Hockey is commonly described as the national sport; this popular designation should not be confused with a constitutional provision.

## 2. Geography

### 2.1 Location and extent

Pakistan lies in South Asia, extending from the Arabian Sea in the south to the high mountain systems of the north. It borders India to the east, Afghanistan to the west and north-west, Iran to the south-west, and China to the north-east. Its southern coastline opens onto the Arabian Sea and the wider Indian Ocean.

### 2.2 Major physical divisions

1. **Northern and north-western mountains:** The Himalaya, Karakoram and Hindu Kush systems meet in the north. The region contains some of the world's highest peaks and large glaciers.
2. **Western mountains and plateaus:** Balochistan's rugged ranges, basins and uplands include the Sulaiman, Kirthar and Makran systems.
3. **Indus plain:** The Indus and its tributaries form a vast alluvial plain through Punjab and Sindh. It is the country's main agricultural and settlement corridor.
4. **Potohar Plateau and Salt Range:** An upland region in northern Punjab with river valleys, ravines, geological formations and historic sites.
5. **Deserts:** The Thar in the south-east, Cholistan in Punjab, Thal in central Punjab and Kharan in Balochistan are important arid landscapes.
6. **Coastal belt:** The Sindh and Makran coasts include beaches, lagoons, mangroves, ports and the rugged Makran Coastal Range.

### 2.3 Mountain ranges and peaks

- **K2 (8,611 m):** The world's second-highest mountain, in the Karakoram.
- **Nanga Parbat (8,126 m):** A major Himalayan peak in the western Himalaya.
- **Gasherbrum I (8,080 m)** and **Broad Peak (8,051 m):** Other major Karakoram peaks.
- **Tirich Mir (7,708 m):** The highest peak of the Hindu Kush.
- **Karakoram, Himalaya and Hindu Kush:** These mountain systems shape climate, glaciers, river systems, biodiversity and high-altitude communities.

### 2.4 Rivers and water systems

The Indus is the principal river system. Its major tributaries include the Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Sutlej and Kabul rivers. The Indus Basin Irrigation System is central to agriculture, drinking-water supply and hydropower. Water availability is shaped by snow and glacier melt, monsoon rainfall, groundwater use, storage infrastructure and transboundary river arrangements.

### 2.5 Lakes, wetlands and coast

Important water bodies include Manchar Lake, Keenjhar (Kalri) Lake, Saif-ul-Malook, Attabad Lake, Satpara Lake and the high-altitude Deosai wetlands. The Indus Delta contains mangrove ecosystems that protect coastal communities and support fisheries. Wetlands are important for migratory birds and are vulnerable to water diversion, pollution and climate pressures.

### 2.6 Climate and seasons

Pakistan has a varied climate: alpine and cold desert conditions in the north; continental conditions across much of the interior; arid and semi-arid climates in Balochistan and Sindh; and a monsoon-influenced summer in many eastern and northern districts. Broad seasons include a cool winter, a hot pre-monsoon period, a summer monsoon season and a post-monsoon transition. Timing and intensity vary by region and year.

### 2.7 Natural resources and hazards

Natural resources include natural gas, coal, copper, gold, rock salt, limestone, chromite, gypsum and agricultural land. Major hazards include river floods, flash floods, glacial lake outburst floods, drought, heatwaves, earthquakes, landslides, cyclones and coastal erosion. Risk is influenced by settlement patterns, infrastructure, land use and preparedness.

## 3. Population, people and languages

### 3.1 Population: census versus estimate

Pakistan's 7th Population and Housing Census, conducted in 2023, recorded **241,499,431 people**. This is a census count, not a current-year estimate. The Pakistan Bureau of Statistics reports the national and administrative-unit tables; future estimates should be labelled by institution and reference year.

### 3.2 Population distribution and cities

Population is concentrated in the Indus plain, especially Punjab and Sindh. Major urban centres include Karachi, Lahore, Faisalabad, Rawalpindi, Gujranwala, Peshawar, Multan, Hyderabad, Quetta and Islamabad. Urban boundaries and metropolitan definitions differ across datasets, so city rankings should always state their source and year.

### 3.3 Languages

Urdu is the national language and a major lingua franca. English is widely used in federal administration, higher education, law and business. Major regional languages include Punjabi, Pashto, Sindhi, Saraiki, Balochi, Brahui, Hindko, Shina, Balti, Kashmiri, Khowar, Wakhi and several smaller languages. Census language categories and linguistic identities do not always map neatly onto one another.

### 3.4 Religion and society

Pakistan was established in the context of the partition of British India and has a constitutional identity as an Islamic republic. The 2023 census and other official sources record a Muslim majority alongside Hindu, Christian and other religious communities. Religious communities are internally diverse. A responsible account should describe constitutional protections, social realities and minority experiences without treating any community as uniform.

### 3.5 Ethnic and regional diversity

Punjabi, Pashtun, Sindhi, Baloch, Saraiki, Muhajir and many other identities contribute to the country's social landscape. These labels can refer to language, region, history or self-identification and should not be treated as fixed or mutually exclusive census categories.

### 3.6 Migration and diaspora

Migration has shaped Pakistan since 1947, including the movement associated with Partition, internal rural-to-urban migration, labour migration to the Gulf and other regions, and a large overseas Pakistani diaspora. Remittances are an important source of foreign exchange and household income.

## 4. History: detailed chronological timeline

### 4.1 Prehistory and early settlement

The territory of present-day Pakistan contains some of South Asia's earliest evidence of settled life and complex societies. Archaeological sites in Balochistan and the Indus region document long histories of farming, craft production, exchange and urban development.

### 4.2 Indus or Harappan Civilisation

The Indus Civilisation flourished in the third millennium BCE, with major urban centres at Mohenjo-daro and Harappa, as well as many settlements across the Indus region. Its cities are noted for planned streets, drainage, craft specialisation, standardised weights and long-distance trade. The script remains undeciphered; interpretations of its political and social organisation remain under study.

### 4.3 Gandhara and ancient northern regions

Gandhara, centred around parts of present-day north-western Pakistan and neighbouring areas, became a major centre of Buddhist learning and art. Taxila and the Buddhist complex at Takht-i-Bahi are key archaeological places. Achaemenid, Greek, Mauryan, Indo-Greek, Kushan and other powers influenced different parts of the region at different times.

### 4.4 Early medieval and regional polities

The region saw the development of local dynasties and shifting political networks, including Hindu Shahi rule in parts of the north-west. Trade routes connected Central Asia, Iran, the Indian subcontinent and the Arabian Sea. Religious and cultural traditions developed through interaction among communities.

### 4.5 Arrival and expansion of Muslim rule

Arab rule was established in Sindh in the early eighth century following the campaign associated with Muhammad bin Qasim. Later, Turkic, Afghan and Persianate dynasties expanded political influence across parts of the region. The Delhi Sultanate and regional sultanates shaped administration, architecture, language and trade.

### 4.6 Mughal period

The Mughal Empire expanded across much of the region from the sixteenth century. Lahore became an important imperial city, and the period left major architectural works, gardens, forts, manuscripts and administrative traditions. Mughal power later weakened as regional states and European companies expanded.

### 4.7 Sikh rule and British expansion

The Sikh Empire under Maharaja Ranjit Singh ruled much of Punjab in the early nineteenth century. Following the Anglo-Sikh wars, the British annexed Punjab in 1849. Sindh had been annexed in 1843, while Balochistan and frontier regions experienced varied forms of treaty-based, indirect and direct colonial control.

### 4.8 Colonial period and political movements

British rule transformed land revenue, irrigation, railways, military recruitment, education and administration. Political movements developed around constitutional reform, representation, identity and self-government. The All-India Muslim League was founded in 1906. Muhammad Ali Jinnah became a leading advocate of the demand for Pakistan.

### 4.9 Pakistan Movement and independence (1940–1947)

The Lahore Resolution of March 1940 became a landmark in the Pakistan Movement. Negotiations, elections, communal conflict and the end of British rule led to the partition of British India. Pakistan became independent on 14 August 1947, initially comprising geographically separated western and eastern wings.

### 4.10 Early state-building and 1947–1971

Partition brought mass migration, displacement and violence, while the new state faced major administrative, economic and institutional challenges. Pakistan adopted its first constitution in 1956 and became an Islamic republic. Political instability and military rule shaped subsequent decades. In 1971, East Pakistan separated after a political and military crisis and became the independent state of Bangladesh.

### 4.11 Constitution of 1973 and later decades

The Constitution of 1973 established a parliamentary federal framework. Pakistan experienced periods of elected civilian government and military rule, constitutional amendments, political transitions and debates over federalism, religion, provincial autonomy and civil-military relations.

### 4.12 Contemporary Pakistan

Contemporary history includes democratic transitions, security challenges, economic restructuring, rapid urbanisation, climate disasters, changing regional relations and a growing digital economy. Historical interpretation remains contested; the article distinguishes established dates and events from political narratives.

## 5. Government, Constitution and administration

### 5.1 Constitutional framework

The Constitution of 1973 is the supreme legal framework. It describes Pakistan as an Islamic Republic and establishes a federal parliamentary system, fundamental rights, institutions of government and the division of legislative responsibilities between the federation and provinces.

### 5.2 Fundamental constitutional features

The Constitution provides for parliamentary government, a President as head of state, a Prime Minister as head of government, a bicameral Parliament, an independent judiciary and provincial governments. Constitutional amendments have changed institutional arrangements over time; legal summaries should be checked against the current consolidated text.

### 5.3 Fundamental rights and principles

The Constitution includes rights relating to life and liberty, safeguards concerning arrest and detention, dignity, freedom of movement, assembly, association, speech, religion and equality, subject to constitutional text and lawful limitations. It also contains Principles of Policy that guide the state.

### 5.4 Federal executive

The President is the constitutional head of state. Executive authority is exercised through the federal government led by the Prime Minister and the Cabinet, which is responsible to the National Assembly under the parliamentary system.

### 5.5 Parliament

The Parliament (Majlis-e-Shoora) consists of the President, the National Assembly and the Senate. The National Assembly is the lower house and is elected by voters. The Senate represents the federating units and is a continuing chamber, with members serving staggered terms.

### 5.6 Judiciary

The judicial system includes the Supreme Court, a Federal Constitutional Court established by constitutional amendment in 2025, High Courts, subordinate courts and specialised tribunals. Because judicial structure and jurisdiction can change through amendments and judgments, readers should consult the current Constitution and official court sources before relying on a legal summary.

### 5.7 Provinces and other administrative areas

Pakistan has four provinces: Balochistan, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Punjab and Sindh. Islamabad is a federal capital territory. Azad Jammu and Kashmir and Gilgit-Baltistan are administered through separate institutional arrangements and should not be casually described as provinces of the federation. District and local-government boundaries change over time.

### 5.8 Elections and institutions

The Election Commission of Pakistan conducts and supervises elections under the Constitution and law. Other important institutions include the State Bank of Pakistan, Auditor-General, Federal Public Service Commission, National Accountability Bureau, Council of Common Interests and National Economic Council. Their legal mandates should be checked against current statutes.

## 6. Economy

### 6.1 Structure

Pakistan has a mixed economy combining agriculture, manufacturing, services, public-sector activity and a large informal economy. Economic performance is influenced by energy costs, external financing, exchange-rate pressures, taxation, productivity, climate risks and global demand.

### 6.2 Agriculture

Agriculture remains important for employment, food security, exports and rural livelihoods. Major crops include wheat, rice, cotton, sugarcane and maize. Livestock is a major component of agricultural output. Irrigation from the Indus Basin is central, while water scarcity, soil degradation, floods and heat stress affect production.

### 6.3 Industry and manufacturing

Important industries include textiles and apparel, food processing, cement, fertiliser, chemicals, pharmaceuticals, steel, leather, sports goods, surgical instruments and automotive assembly. Sialkot is internationally known for sports goods and surgical instruments. Industrial competitiveness depends on energy reliability, skills, logistics, technology and export access.

### 6.4 Services

Services include wholesale and retail trade, transport, finance, telecommunications, education, health, public administration and information technology. Digital services and freelancing have become visible parts of the economy, though skills, infrastructure and reliable connectivity remain uneven.

### 6.5 Banking and monetary system

The State Bank of Pakistan is the central bank. It manages monetary policy, currency issuance, financial stability and regulation within its statutory mandate. Pakistan's currency is the Pakistani rupee (PKR). Inflation, exchange-rate changes and interest rates affect households and businesses.

### 6.6 Trade and infrastructure

Exports include textiles, apparel, rice, leather goods, sports goods, surgical instruments, chemicals and other manufactured or agricultural products. Imports include energy, machinery, chemicals, edible oils, transport equipment and industrial inputs. Karachi Port, Port Qasim and Gwadar are important maritime facilities; roads, railways and logistics corridors connect production centres to markets.

### 6.7 Economic challenges and opportunities

Challenges include low tax mobilisation, external debt and financing needs, energy-sector circular debt, water stress, informality, uneven education and skills, and climate vulnerability. Opportunities include agricultural productivity, renewable energy, mineral development with safeguards, export diversification, tourism, logistics and digital services.

## 7. Culture, heritage and everyday life

### 7.1 Cultural diversity

Pakistan's culture reflects regional languages, Islamic traditions, older South Asian and Central Asian histories, Sufi networks, migration and local customs. Cultural practices differ across provinces, cities, rural communities and generations.

### 7.2 Languages and literature

Urdu literature includes poetry, fiction, drama and criticism. Important literary traditions also exist in Punjabi, Sindhi, Pashto, Balochi, Saraiki, Brahui and northern languages. Allama Muhammad Iqbal is a major poet and philosopher; Faiz Ahmed Faiz, Saadat Hasan Manto, Shah Abdul Latif Bhittai, Bulleh Shah, Khushal Khan Khattak and many others are central to regional and national literary histories.

### 7.3 Music

Musical traditions include qawwali, ghazal, kafi, folk music, classical music, regional instruments and contemporary pop and rock. Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan helped bring qawwali to international audiences. Regional traditions include Sindhi, Balochi, Pashto, Punjabi and Seraiki forms.

### 7.4 Architecture and crafts

The built heritage includes Indus Civilisation cities, Gandharan Buddhist sites, Mughal forts and gardens, Sikh-era monuments, colonial civic buildings and modern architecture. Crafts include truck art, ajrak printing, Sindhi embroidery, Balochi needlework, pottery, woodwork, carpets, ceramics and metalwork.

### 7.5 Food

Food varies by region and household. Common dishes include biryani, nihari, karahi, haleem, chapli kebab, sajji, saag, daal, roti, naan and a wide range of rice and meat preparations. Coastal cuisines use fish and seafood; northern regions have distinct breads, soups and meat dishes. Food traditions are not uniform across the country.

### 7.6 Festivals and observances

Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha are major religious festivals. Ramadan shapes daily routines for many Muslims. Pakistan Day (23 March), Independence Day (14 August), Iqbal Day and other national or provincial observances are marked in different ways. Dates of Islamic festivals follow the lunar calendar.

### 7.7 Clothing and textiles

Shalwar kameez is widely worn, with considerable regional variation in cut, fabric and embroidery. Ajrak, Sindhi caps, Balochi embroidery, Punjabi phulkari traditions and northern woollens are among the regionally distinctive textile practices.

### 7.8 Sports

Cricket is the most widely followed sport. Field hockey has a major historical place in Pakistan's sporting identity, including Olympic and World Cup successes. Squash champions such as Jahangir Khan and Jansher Khan achieved international prominence. Football, kabaddi, wrestling, polo and mountaineering also have important communities.

## 8. Education, science and technology

### 8.1 Education system

Education is delivered through federal, provincial, public, private and community institutions. The system includes early childhood, primary, middle, secondary, higher secondary, technical and vocational education, and universities. Access, learning outcomes, gender gaps, teacher training and regional inequalities remain important policy issues.

### 8.2 Major universities and institutions

Examples include the University of the Punjab, University of Karachi, Quaid-i-Azam University, University of Peshawar, NED University of Engineering and Technology, National University of Sciences and Technology, Aga Khan University and Lahore University of Management Sciences. Institutional status, rankings and programme offerings should be checked with the relevant regulator and university.

### 8.3 Science and research

Research spans agriculture, medicine, engineering, materials, climate, water, computing and public health. The Pakistan Academy of Sciences and Higher Education Commission support parts of the national research ecosystem. Research capacity varies across institutions and disciplines.

### 8.4 Space and technology

The Space and Upper Atmosphere Research Commission (SUPARCO) is Pakistan's national space agency. Pakistan has launched and operated satellites for communications, remote sensing and scientific purposes. The country's technology sector includes software services, telecommunications, fintech, e-commerce and a growing start-up ecosystem.

### 8.5 Medicine and public health

Pakistan's health system includes federal and provincial public services, private hospitals, primary-care facilities, teaching hospitals and community programmes. Major public-health priorities include maternal and child health, immunisation, nutrition, infectious diseases, non-communicable diseases, clean water and equitable access to care.

## 9. Tourism, monuments and natural wonders

### 9.1 UNESCO World Heritage

UNESCO's World Heritage Centre lists **six inscribed properties** in Pakistan (all cultural in the current list consulted):

1. Archaeological Ruins at Moenjodaro (1980)
2. Taxila (1980)
3. Buddhist Ruins of Takht-i-Bahi and Neighbouring City Remains at Sahr-i-Bahlol (1980)
4. Fort and Shalamar Gardens in Lahore (1981)
5. Historical Monuments at Makli, Thatta (1981)
6. Rohtas Fort (1997)

UNESCO's Tentative List is a separate category; a tentative property is not yet an inscribed World Heritage Site.

### 9.2 Major historic places

- **Mohenjo-daro:** Major urban centre of the Indus Civilisation in Sindh.
- **Harappa:** Key archaeological site in Punjab.
- **Taxila:** Historic centre of learning and Gandharan archaeology.
- **Lahore Fort and Shalimar Gardens:** Major Mughal heritage complex.
- **Makli Necropolis:** Vast historic funerary landscape near Thatta.
- **Rohtas Fort:** Fortification built under Sher Shah Suri.
- **Badshahi Mosque and Wazir Khan Mosque:** Important Mughal-era architecture in Lahore.
- **Baltit and Altit forts:** Heritage sites in Hunza.
- **Derawar Fort:** Landmark of the Cholistan desert.

### 9.3 Natural attractions

Notable landscapes include Hunza and the Karakoram Highway, Skardu, Deosai National Park, Saif-ul-Malook Lake, Naltar Valley, Fairy Meadows, Swat Valley, Neelum Valley, Hingol National Park, the Makran coast, Ziarat's juniper forests, the Thar Desert and the Indus Delta. Access, permits, weather, security and conservation rules should be checked before travel.

### 9.4 Protected areas and wildlife

Pakistan's protected-area network includes national parks, wildlife sanctuaries, game reserves, marine and coastal habitats and community conservation areas. Examples include Khunjerab, Deosai, Central Karakoram, Hingol, Kirthar, Chitral Gol and Ayubia national parks. Protected-area lists and boundaries change; consult provincial wildlife departments and the national biodiversity clearing-house for current registers.

## 10. National identity, institutions and international relations

### 10.1 International memberships

Pakistan is a member of the United Nations, Organisation of Islamic Cooperation, South Asian Association for Regional Cooperation, Shanghai Cooperation Organisation, Commonwealth and other international and regional bodies. Membership and participation should be checked against each organisation's current official records.

### 10.2 Foreign policy themes

Foreign policy has been shaped by geography, security, trade, relations with neighbouring countries, ties with China, the United States, Gulf states and the wider Muslim world, and engagement with multilateral institutions. Relations with India, Afghanistan and Iran are particularly influenced by border, security, water, trade and regional stability issues. These relationships change over time and should be described with dated sources.

### 10.3 Armed forces

Pakistan's armed forces comprise the Army, Navy and Air Force. The armed forces have played a significant role in the country's security and political history. Civil-military relations, defence policy and nuclear governance are important subjects for balanced study.

### 10.4 National days

- **Pakistan Day:** 23 March, associated with the Lahore Resolution of 1940 and the adoption of the first Constitution in 1956.
- **Independence Day:** 14 August.
- **Defence Day:** 6 September.
- **Iqbal Day:** 9 November, observed in recognition of Allama Iqbal (official holiday status may vary by year).

## 11. Environment, biodiversity and conservation

### 11.1 Ecosystems

Pakistan includes alpine meadows, glaciers, conifer forests, dry temperate forests, subtropical scrub, riverine forests, irrigated plains, deserts, wetlands, mangroves and marine habitats. The country's ecological diversity is strongly linked to altitude and water availability.

### 11.2 Iconic wildlife

Species associated with Pakistan include the markhor, snow leopard, Indus river dolphin, Himalayan brown bear, ibex, urial, Marco Polo sheep, houbara bustard, green turtles and a wide variety of migratory birds. Conservation status differs by species and population.

### 11.3 Conservation institutions

Federal and provincial environment, forestry and wildlife departments manage protected areas and conservation programmes. Community-based conservation has been important in parts of the north. International frameworks include the Convention on Biological Diversity, Ramsar Convention and Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species (CITES).

### 11.4 Environmental pressures

Key pressures include water scarcity, groundwater depletion, air pollution, deforestation, habitat fragmentation, plastic and solid-waste pollution, soil salinity, coastal degradation and biodiversity loss. Rapid urbanisation and industrial growth increase the importance of effective planning and environmental regulation.

### 11.5 Climate change

Pakistan is highly exposed to climate hazards, including extreme heat, intense rainfall, river and flash floods, drought, glacier-related hazards and sea-level rise. The 2022 floods demonstrated the scale of social and economic impacts. Adaptation priorities include early-warning systems, resilient infrastructure, water management, climate-smart agriculture, ecosystem restoration and disaster preparedness.

## 12. Public health, transport and infrastructure

### 12.1 Public health

Public health responsibilities are shared across federal and provincial institutions. Key areas include primary healthcare, immunisation, maternal and child health, nutrition, disease surveillance, sanitation and access to essential medicines. Health indicators must be cited with their year and source.

### 12.2 Transport

Pakistan's transport network includes national highways, motorways, railways, urban transit, domestic aviation and maritime ports. Pakistan Railways operates the national rail network. Major airports serve Karachi, Lahore, Islamabad, Peshawar, Quetta and other cities. Mountain roads such as the Karakoram Highway are strategically and economically important but can be affected by weather and landslides.

### 12.3 Energy

The energy mix includes natural gas, oil, coal, hydropower, nuclear power, solar and wind. Energy affordability, transmission capacity, fuel imports, circular debt and reliable access are persistent policy concerns. Renewable-energy deployment and grid modernisation are important areas for future development.

### 12.4 Communications and digital infrastructure

Mobile networks, broadband, fibre links, data centres, digital payments and e-government services support the country's growing digital economy. Access and quality vary by geography, income and infrastructure.

## 16. Glossary of essential terms

- **Azad Jammu and Kashmir (AJK):** A territory administered by Pakistan with its own government and distinct constitutional status; its international status is disputed.
- **Balochistan:** Pakistan's largest province by area.
- **Gandhara:** An ancient cultural-historical region associated with parts of north-western South Asia.
- **Gilgit-Baltistan:** A mountainous region administered by Pakistan with a distinct governance arrangement.
- **Indus Basin:** The river system and drainage basin centred on the Indus and its tributaries.
- **Indus Civilisation:** Bronze Age urban civilisation also called the Harappan Civilisation.
- **Karakoram:** High mountain range in northern Pakistan and neighbouring regions.
- **Majlis-e-Shoora:** The constitutional name for Pakistan's Parliament.
- **Monsoon:** Seasonal wind and rainfall system influencing parts of South Asia.
- **PKR:** ISO currency code for the Pakistani rupee.
- **Province:** A federating unit of Pakistan.
- **Ramsar site:** A wetland designated under the Ramsar Convention.
- **SUPARCO:** Space and Upper Atmosphere Research Commission, Pakistan's national space agency.
- **Tentative List:** A country's inventory of sites it may consider nominating to UNESCO's World Heritage List; not an inscription.
- **Urdu:** Pakistan's national language and an important lingua franca.
- **UTC+5:** Coordinated Universal Time offset used by Pakistan Standard Time.
